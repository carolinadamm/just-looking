a puzzle about what "aesthetic" could mean

How to play

  1. Consult your gut. How do you define "aesthetic(s)" intuitively?
  2. Complete the puzzle.
  3. Under some puzzle pieces, I hid different authors' thoughts about the term "aesthetic". Make sure to reach all of them, and read a second time if the wording is tricky (spoiler: it is).
  4. All quotes are ripped out of their original context and the connection to aesthetics may not be apparent at first sight. Many of these authors have written entire books about their ideas. You are expected to be a bit puzzled.
  5. Take notes. What questions are the quotes trying to answer? Are the statements in harmony, or do you see contradictions? What patterns do you identify? Who is being quoted and why does that matter? Also, who is not quoted?

Positionality

I am Lotte Damm, an undergraduate student preparing a tutorial about digital x philosophy of aesthetics. I made this puzzle to share my excitement about the topic. I also chose this image deliberately: I dislike Sabrina Carpenter, and I wanted to see what happens when a pop image I find unconvincing gets taken apart and put back together next to some very old and very serious ideas about beauty. My selection of quotes is partial and shaped by what I could access and read. Who is missing is part of the question.

A note on anthropology and a contested field

Some quotes here come from anthropology, ethnography and writing about non-Western and Indigenous art. This means we are stepping into a contested field, and anthropology grew up alongside colonialism. Quoting an author is not endorsing them, and I do not support every quote. Please read with care, and ask yourself:

Who is speaking in each quote, and about whom? Who is being spoken for?

Where do you notice language that denounces, flattens or dehumanises people? What would you need to know to be sure?

Whose experiences count as "aesthetic", and who decided that?

What happens when one tradition's concept is applied to everyone else's practices? Is it still the same concept?

What would it mean to read these texts carefully without simply accepting or dismissing them?
